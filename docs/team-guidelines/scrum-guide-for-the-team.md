# Agile Scrum: A Beginner's Guide for Our Team

This guide explains what Scrum is, why we're using it, and how our day-to-day work will change. No prior knowledge is needed.

---

## 1. First, what is "Agile"?

**Agile** is a way of working that delivers value in **small, frequent pieces** instead of one big delivery at the end.

Think about building a car for a customer:

- **Traditional approach ("Waterfall"):** plan everything up front, build for a year, deliver the finished car. If the customer's needs changed in month three, we find out too late.
- **Agile approach:** first deliver a skateboard, then a scooter, then a bike, then a car. At every step the customer can use something, give feedback, and help us steer.

Agile is based on four core values (from the *Agile Manifesto*, 2001):

1. **People and interactions** over processes and tools
2. **Working results** over exhaustive documentation
3. **Collaboration with the customer** over rigid contracts
4. **Responding to change** over following a fixed plan

---

## 2. What is Scrum?

**Scrum** is the most popular *framework* for putting Agile into practice. It gives us a simple structure:

- **3 roles** (who does what)
- **5 events** (when we meet and why)
- **3 artifacts** (what we keep track of)

The heart of Scrum is the **Sprint**: a fixed period of time, usually **1 to 4 weeks** (most teams choose 2), during which the team builds a usable, finished piece of work. Then we repeat, learning and improving each time.

> **Key idea:** Scrum doesn't try to predict everything in advance. It works in short cycles, checks results often, and adapts. We call this **inspect and adapt**.

---

## 3. The Three Roles

### Product Owner (PO)
The person responsible for **what** the team builds and **why**.
- Owns and prioritizes the Product Backlog (the to-do list, see below)
- Represents customers and stakeholders
- Decides what is most valuable to work on next
- Is available to answer the team's questions

### Scrum Master (SM)
The person responsible for **how well Scrum works**. Think of a coach, not a boss.
- Helps the team understand and follow Scrum
- Facilitates the meetings (events)
- Removes obstacles ("impediments") that slow the team down
- Protects the team from interruptions during the Sprint

### Developers (the Team)
The people who **do the work**. "Developers" doesn't only mean programmers: it includes anyone creating the product (designers, testers, analysts, writers...).
- Decide **how** to do the work
- Commit to a goal for each Sprint
- Are self-organizing: nobody assigns tasks from above; the team organizes itself

> The Scrum Master is **not** a project manager giving orders, and the Product Owner does **not** tell developers how to do their job.

---

## 4. The Three Artifacts

### Product Backlog
An ordered list of **everything** that could be done for the product: features, improvements, bug fixes. The most important items are at the top. It's never "finished": it evolves constantly.

Items are often written as **User Stories**:

> *As a* **[type of user]**, *I want* **[something]**, *so that* **[benefit]**.
>
> Example: *As a customer, I want to reset my password by email, so that I can access my account if I forget it.*

### Sprint Backlog
The items the team selected from the Product Backlog **for the current Sprint**, plus the plan to complete them. It belongs to the Developers.

### Increment
The **finished, usable result** produced by the end of the Sprint. Each Increment adds to the previous ones.

#### Definition of Done (DoD)
A shared checklist that says when a piece of work is truly "done". Example:
- Code written and reviewed
- Tests passed
- Documentation updated
- Approved by the Product Owner

If it doesn't meet the DoD, it's **not done**, and it doesn't count.

## 5. The Five Events

All events are **timeboxed**: they have a maximum duration and we respect it. Durations below assume a 2-week Sprint.

### 1. The Sprint (the container)
The fixed-length cycle (e.g. 2 weeks) that contains all other events. A new Sprint starts right after the previous one ends.

### 2. Sprint Planning — *start of the Sprint, max ~4 hours*
The whole team decides:
- **Why** is this Sprint valuable? → we define a **Sprint Goal**
- **What** can we complete? → we pick items from the top of the Product Backlog
- **How** will we do it? → we break items into tasks

### 3. Daily Scrum — *every day, max 15 minutes*
A short, same-time-same-place meeting for the Developers to sync up. A common format is three questions:
1. What did I do yesterday toward the Sprint Goal?
2. What will I do today?
3. Is anything blocking me?

It's **not** a status report to a manager, and it's not the place to solve problems in detail: discussions happen afterward with only the people involved.

### 4. Sprint Review — *end of the Sprint, max ~2 hours*
The team **shows what it built** (a live demo, not slides) to the Product Owner and stakeholders. Everyone gives feedback, and the Product Backlog may be updated as a result.

### 5. Sprint Retrospective — *after the Review, max ~1.5 hours*
The team reflects on **how it worked**, not on the product:
- What went well?
- What didn't go well?
- What will we try to improve in the next Sprint?

The team leaves with 1–3 concrete actions. This is how we get better over time.

---

## 6. How It All Fits Together

```
        Product Backlog  (ordered by the Product Owner)
               │
               ▼
      ┌──── Sprint Planning ────┐
      │   → Sprint Goal          │
      │   → Sprint Backlog       │
      └──────────┬───────────────┘
                 ▼
     ┌──────── THE SPRINT (e.g. 2 weeks) ────────┐
     │  Work every day + Daily Scrum (15 min)     │
     └──────────┬─────────────────────────────────┘
                 ▼
          Increment (Done!)
                 │
                 ▼
          Sprint Review  → feedback on the PRODUCT
                 │
                 ▼
       Sprint Retrospective → improve the PROCESS
                 │
                 └──────► next Sprint begins
```

## 7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Turning the Daily Scrum into a long status meeting.** Keep it to 15 minutes.
- **Adding work mid-Sprint.** Changes go into the Product Backlog for the next Sprint (unless there's a real emergency, discussed with the PO).
- **Skipping the Retrospective** because "we're busy". It's how we stop repeating the same problems.
- **Calling work "done" when it isn't.** Respect the Definition of Done.
- **Using velocity to judge people.** Scrum is about the team, not individual performance.
- **Doing "Scrum in name only".** Holding the meetings isn't enough: the values matter.

---

## 8. The Scrum Values

Scrum works when the team lives these five values:

- **Commitment:** we commit to our goals and to each other
- **Focus:** we concentrate on the Sprint's work
- **Openness:** we are transparent about our work and our problems
- **Respect:** we respect each other as capable people
- **Courage:** we do the right thing and tackle tough problems
